About

Boards, firmware, and the awkward gaps between them.

I’m a second-year electrical and computer engineering student at UT Austin, studying in the Honors program with a minor in Business. I like problems that start with something physical — a magnet, a sensor, a bare board — and refuse to be finished until there’s something honest coming out the other end.

Most engineers own one layer of a system. I keep ending up responsible for all of them, and the seams turned out to be the interesting part: a board that has to survive the firmware written for it, a protocol two teams have to share, a dataset quietly deciding whether a model was ever going to work. Nobody owns the seams by default, which is exactly why they break.

Right now I’m a software engineering intern at GE Vernova, doing electronics for Longhorn Baja Racing, and researching wireless strain sensors that get sealed inside 3D-printed metal. I’m looking for a Summer 2027 internship in hardware, embedded, or robotics.

Most of what I know, I learned by getting it wrong on something with a lead time.
